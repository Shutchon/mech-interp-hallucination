\begin{summary}
Small language models (SLMs) are increasingly deployed in resource-constrained
systems, yet they remain prone to factual hallucination, particularly when a
misleading context conflicts with parametric knowledge (a context--memory
conflict). We present a systematic activation-patching study of this conflict in
three SLMs (\emodel{} with 2{,}000 facts, Llama-3.2-1B and
Qwen2.5-1.5B with 600 facts each), each filtered to facts the model demonstrably knows.
Restoration effects concentrate in the final quarter of the network
(pairwise Spearman correlation of layer profiles 0.81--1.00), dominated
by a small set of late-layer attention heads. Bidirectional patching shows that influential heads act in
both patching directions (head-level superposition) and separates
dual-role heads from the specialist head whose neutral-control
effect is minimal. In \emodel{}, knocking out that specialist, L22.H4
(the only head, of the four checked in both bfloat16 and float32,
passing the neutral criterion), reduces
misleading-context distractor-following by 35\% relative (exact McNemar
$p{=}3.4\times10^{-23}$) while
increasing first-token accuracy by 5.3 percentage points, with
MMLU retention 0.992, a 5\% perplexity cost, and accuracy under a
supportive correct context nearly intact (96.1\% vs.\ 98.2\%); in the two replication models, single-head knockouts of each
model's own dev-ranked head reduce distractor-following by 30--50\%
(the specialist characterization itself is \emodel{}-only). Eight of ten random single-head knockouts beat the
specialist on both behavioral metrics at comparable capability cost;
two carry severe costs (retention 0.70; PPL $\times$2.3). The work's
contribution is thus causal localization, role characterization, and
measured trade-offs, not a demonstrated superiority of the selection
procedure over random single-head ablation. We report
quantified dose--response
trade-offs between reduction and capability, with relation-level splits
guarding against circular analysis.
\end{summary}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% Narrative: SLM deployment -> hallucination under misleading context is a
% systems problem -> gap -> contributions.

Language models of 1--2.6B parameters now power on-device
assistants~\cite{llama32card}, retrieval-augmented
pipelines~\cite{aromsuk2026rag}, and document-processing systems
where latency and memory budgets preclude larger models. In such deployments the
model must frequently integrate an external context with its parametric
knowledge. When the context asserts a false fact (whether through a retrieval error, an adversarial
document, or a mistaken user, one way misinformation reaches a model
in context~\cite{mishima2022fake}), the model
faces a \emph{context--memory (CM) conflict}~\cite{xu2024knowledge}, and
resolving it incorrectly produces a factual hallucination, which can then cascade into further errors~\cite{zhang2024snowball}.

Why a mechanistic account? Deployed systems need interventions that
are cheap, targeted, and auditable: knowing \emph{which} components
carry the conflict lets a builder trade hallucination against
capability with measured confidence. The account is more valuable still
if the responsible components transfer across architectures, which is
why replication is a first-class concern here.

A growing body of work studies hallucination mechanisms, including
knowledge-deficit failures~\cite{yu2024mechanistic} and
probes/directions for truthfulness~\cite{li2023itti,marks2024geometry}.
Head-level analyses of CM conflict exist in text-only
models~\cite{li2025juice,jin2024ph3} and multimodal
settings~\cite{jiang2026causal}. What has been missing
for this size class is a systematic causal account (which components, located how, trade hallucination
against capability when intervened upon, and whether findings
replicate across architectures), and, for head-level work generally, a neutral-control separation of head roles
and quantified single-head intervention costs.

This paper provides that account with a design that separates localization
from validation: heads are selected only on a development split of
relations, and all intervention results are reported on held-out relations.
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Localization.} A coarse-to-fine activation-patching map
of the CM-conflict circuit in \emodel{}, replicated in two other
architectures: restoration concentrates in late layers, and
attention patches restore more than MLP patches under our protocol
(positions differ across corruption regimes), a protocol-bound
contrast with the MLP-heavy picture of classical fact recall.
\item \textbf{Characterization.} Bidirectional patching quantifies
per-head asymmetry and separates dual-role from
conflict-specialist heads via the neutral control.
\item \textbf{Quantified trade-offs.} Dose--response curves for knockout
and mean-ablation with fairness baselines: patching-guided selection
reduces hallucination significantly (exact McNemar $p{<}10^{-6}$ for
single heads in \emodel{}; $p{=}0.004$/$9{\times}10^{-10}$ in the
replications), norm-based selection \emph{increases} it, and capability
costs dissociate (MMLU $\approx$1.00; perplexity +5--20\%).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Hallucination and knowledge conflict.} Surveys taxonomize
hallucination in NLG~\cite{ji2023survey} and knowledge conflicts in
LLMs~\cite{xu2024knowledge}. Behavioral studies quantify when context
overrides memory~\cite{du2024context}; attention-map analysis detects
contextual hallucinations~\cite{chuang2024lookback} and enables
quality-improving masking in MT~\cite{cai2025attention}; data-side
remedies refine noisy factual resources~\cite{dong2026kg}.
Yu et al.\ trace non-factual hallucination to knowledge deficits in
MLPs and selection failures in attention~\cite{yu2024mechanistic}; our
corruption regime instead targets \emph{knows-but-overridden} failures.
Mechanistic work has also examined conflicts inside parametric memory
itself~\cite{pham2026intra} and cautioned that heads appearing to
implement factual recall may partly implement generic
copying~\cite{campregher2025}, a confound our neutral control
tests for at the level of patching effects between conditions (not a
pathway isolation).

\textbf{Activation patching and circuits.} Causal mediation introduced the
method~\cite{vig2020bias}; ROME localized factual associations with causal
tracing~\cite{meng2022rome}; best practices are systematized
in~\cite{zhang2024bestpractices}. Head-level analyses include
IOI~\cite{wang2023ioi} and copy suppression~\cite{mcdougall2023copy}.
Closest to us are two head-level studies of knowledge conflict. JuICE
reports that influential heads play superposed dual roles in knowledge
conflicts~\cite{li2025juice}; PH3 identifies memory and context heads by
path patching and prunes them to steer conflict resolution in either
direction, demonstrating cross-model, cross-relation, and cross-format
transfer~\cite{jin2024ph3}. Our work differs in what is selected and how
the cost is measured: (i) a neutral-control analysis that separates
conflict specialists from dual-role heads, a distinction neither method
draws; (ii) single-head specialist knockout selected and evaluated under
a relation-disjoint dev/held-out split; and (iii) explicit capability
trade-off curves (MMLU, perplexity) reported alongside hallucination
metrics, which, to our knowledge, neither study reports. Our top-$k$ knockout is
structurally close to PH3-style context-head pruning; specialist
selection removes 35\% of distractor-following with one head while
accuracy rises, whereas aggregate pruning trades accuracy for
compliance (Sect.~\ref{sec:results-interventions}).

\textbf{Inference-time intervention.} ITI steers along probe-identified
truthful directions~\cite{li2023itti}; truth directions are linearly
represented~\cite{marks2024geometry}; hallucination can also be detected
from sampling uncertainty~\cite{farquhar2024semantic}. Our interventions
are \emph{subtractive} (head knockout/mean-ablation) and selected by causal
patching rather than probes, with explicit capability trade-off curves.

\textbf{Attention sinks and knowledge editing.} Autoregressive models
allocate disproportionate attention to initial tokens
(``attention sinks''), which stabilize generation but are usually treated
as functionally inert no-ops~\cite{xiao2024sinks}. Our findings complicate
this picture: the conflict-specialist heads that dominate the CM-conflict circuit
are BOS-sink heads that are causally \emph{active} under misleading
context (Sect.~\ref{sec:results-superposition}). Separately, knowledge
editing locates and rewrites stored facts~\cite{meng2022rome,yao2023editing};
we instead locate components involved in \emph{context override} and
ablate them at inference time, leaving parameters untouched.

\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Models.} Primary: \emodel{}~(26 layers, 8 heads)~\cite{gemma2024}. Cross-model:
Llama-3.2-1B (16L/32H)~\cite{llama32card,llama2024}, Qwen2.5-1.5B (28L/12H)~\cite{qwen2025}. All experiments use
bfloat16 on a single 24GB GPU. Two separate numerical checks: (i)
\emph{framework parity}: our hooking framework vs.\ reference
HuggingFace logits, both in float32 (mean KL $1.3\times10^{-5}$,
argmax agreement 100\%); (ii) \emph{precision robustness} of the
reported metrics themselves (Sect.~\ref{sec:results-localization}).

\textbf{Data.} From CounterFact~\cite{meng2022rome} (first 12{,}000
facts of the \texttt{wangzn2001/counteract} copy, case order) we
retain facts each
model demonstrably knows (first-token argmax over the full vocabulary
equals the first token of the space-prefixed true target, and the
first-token logit gap over the counterfactual distractor $\ge 0.5$),
sampling 2{,}000 facts from the 2{,}130 that pass both filters
(17.8\% filter survival; 16.7\% of scored facts retained; seed 0),
split 807/1{,}193
dev/held-out \emph{by relation}, so the held-out split shares no relation
with the split used for any selection. A CM-conflict suite asserts the
distractor in three templates; a neutral control replaces it with a
non-answer token.

\textbf{Metrics and definitions.} Let $z_t(x)$ and $z_d(x)$ denote the
logits, at the answer position of input $x$, of the first tokens of the
true target and the distractor, respectively. The \emph{logit difference}
is $\mathrm{LD}(x) = z_t(x) - z_d(x)$. Let $c$, $m$, and $n$ denote the
clean, misleading, and neutral prompts for a given fact, and let
$h$ index a specific attention head. Writing
$x \leftarrow y_h$ for the input $x$ with head $h$'s activation replaced
by its value from input $y$, the three directed patching effects are:
\begin{align}
D &= \mathrm{LD}(c) - \mathrm{LD}(m), \label{eq:denom} \\
A_h &= \frac{\mathrm{LD}(m \leftarrow c_h) - \mathrm{LD}(m)}{D},
\label{eq:A} \\
B_h &= \frac{\mathrm{LD}(c) - \mathrm{LD}(c \leftarrow m_h)}{D},
\label{eq:B} \\
N_h &= \frac{\mathrm{LD}(n \leftarrow c_h) - \mathrm{LD}(n)}
{\mathrm{LD}(c) - \mathrm{LD}(n)}.
\label{eq:N}
\end{align}
Here $A_h$ is the \emph{restore effect} (patching the clean value
into the misleading run; $A{=}1$ is full restoration), $B_h$ the
\emph{context-push effect} (patching the misleading value into the
clean run; $B{=}1$ is full degradation), and $N_h$ the \emph{neutral
effect} (patch into the neutral run, normalized by the clean--neutral
gap, not $D$ of Eq.\,(\ref{eq:denom}) used for $A$ and $B$).
The \emph{directional asymmetry index} $S = |A-B|/(|A|+|B|+\epsilon)$,
with $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$, equals 1 for a head that pushes one direction
only and 0 for a balanced dual-role head; low $S$ thus corresponds to
strong superposition in the sense of~\cite{li2025juice}, and we use the
two framings interchangeably below. When both $|A|$ and $|B|$ are near zero
the index is uninformative, and we exclude such heads from
superposition-based claims. Effects where the denominator has magnitude
$< 10^{-6}$ are set to 0. Head-level values of $A$, $B$, and $N$ are
means over per-fact effects; the reported neutral ratio $N/A$ is the
ratio of these means (not a mean of per-fact ratios). Hallucination
rate is the fraction of misleading-prompt items whose greedy completion
equals the distractor (context-following); accuracy is the fraction
matching the true target; both are first-token measures, so
``accuracy'' means first-token accuracy throughout. We use the term
``misleading-context distractor-following'' for this specific,
programmatically verifiable metric, which is a strict subset of
open-ended factual hallucination. We use ``circuit'' to mean the
causally localized head set, not a fully traced pathway.

\textbf{Protocol.} Stage~1 (coarse): patch residual-stream block
outputs layer-by-layer under two corruptions (ROME-style embedding noise and the misleading
context), measuring normalized restoration of
the true-vs-distractor logit difference. Stage~2 decomposes
attention vs.\ MLP outputs on top layers; Stage~3 patches per-head
$z$-vectors; Stage~4 characterizes each top head bidirectionally
(Eqs.\,(\ref{eq:A})--(\ref{eq:N})); Stage~5 zeroes or mean-ablates
selected heads and evaluates hallucination, accuracy, and neutral
accuracy on held-out items, with MMLU (2{,}000-question subset) and
WikiText-2 perplexity (1024-token windows) under the same hooks. Statistics: bootstrap 95\% CIs from 10{,}000 resamples at the
fact level (clustering the templates of one fact together); two-sided exact binomial McNemar tests against
``none'', where $b$ counts items the intervention fixes and $c$ items it
breaks (per outcome: distractor-following, truth, neutral, or
correct-context accuracy). The primary comparison is \spec{} vs.\ none; all other
configurations are exploratory.

\textbf{Reproducibility details.} Misleading templates (distractor
$d$, prompt $p$): T0 = ``\emph{Earlier you learned that the correct
answer is $d$.~$p$}''; T1 = ``\emph{The document states: $p$~$d$. Now
answer correctly:~$p$}''; T2 = ``\emph{Hint from the user: I am sure
it is $d$.~$p$}''; the neutral control replaces $d$ with ``unknown''.
The relation-level split uses seed~0: 31 relations, 16 dev (807 facts)
and 15 held-out (1{,}193), zero overlap.
Answers are scored by first-token argmax at the final position against
the first token of the space-prefixed targets (no autoregressive
generation, hence no whitespace/punctuation/alias handling). All
retained targets are single words (a verified property, not a filter)
and no pair shares a first word, so the comparison is well-defined;
known-fact status uses the same level.
A greedy 8-token completion check on 300 held-out items (template
T0, first 300 in case order, relation-clustered and hence an easier
subset with a higher baseline than the intervention tables in
Sect.~\ref{sec:results-interventions}; the check validates scoring
coincidence, not aggregate rates) reproduces
first-token accuracy exactly (none 82.3\%, \spec{} 83.7\%; matching
compares the leading word of the continuation, case- and
punctuation-stripped, with the target; this is a leading-word check, not open-ended
factual accuracy). No true/distractor pair shares a first
token: the argmax-plus-gap filter would score such a pair 0 and drop it. Patching positions: the noise regime patches every non-BOS position of
the identically-lengthened runs; the misleading regime and head-level
patching use the last three tokens, right-aligned across runs. Noise
corruption is Gaussian at the embedding layer ($\sigma=3\times$
embedding std, all non-BOS tokens, one draw per case). The top-norm
baseline ranks heads by mean absolute $z$-component (averaged over
token positions and the head dimension, then over 64 dev bare-fact
prompts); the
mean-ablation vector is each head's mean $z$ over those prompts pooled
across token positions, substituted at all positions during
evaluation.
Six of the top-10 restore heads pass $|N|{<}0.01$; among these
qualifiers, \spec{} ranks first on \emph{both} restore effect ($A$,
0.071) and context-push effect ($B$, 0.143), making it the unambiguous
top specialist regardless of which metric is used for selection
(the overall restore leader, L25.H4, is excluded by the neutral
criterion). spec3 takes the three highest-$A$ qualifiers (\spec{},
L22.H0, L20.H3); rank-based choices, no further threshold. Cross-model profile correlation
interpolates each model's per-layer mean-effect profile at 10 evenly
spaced depth fractions (0.05--0.95) before Spearman ranking. Head
rankings and Stage-4 selection use template T0; random-baseline heads
are five draws from the full-head pool followed by five from the
late-layer pool (layers 20--25), one continuous RNG sequence (seed
20261014; per-head results in Sect.~\ref{sec:results-interventions}),
while random-5
uses five separate seeds. Coarse/fine sweeps take the first dev facts in case order (caps
300/150); Stage~4 patches one suffix position at a time (150 dev
facts $\times$ 3 positions $=$ 450 fact--position records), averaged
to head level over records, with bootstrap resampling at the fact
level (positions travel with their fact). The replication models apply the same filters with a 600-fact cap
sampled from survivor pools of 3{,}860 (Llama; 28 relations, 14/14
dev/held-out) and 3{,}151 (Qwen; 28 relations, 14/14). All sweeps
take the first dev cases in \texttt{case\_id} order, a fixed deterministic policy, though case order can correlate with relation
blocks. All runs use a single NVIDIA L4 (24\,GB). Relation and case
IDs per split, head lists for every configuration, MMLU question IDs,
per-item records, analysis scripts, and exact package versions are
provided in the supplementary material archived with this submission.
Models use
HuggingFace checkpoints \texttt{google/gemma-2-2b},
\texttt{meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B}, \texttt{Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B}
(base, not instruct-tuned) loaded in bfloat16 via TransformerLens~2.18
with \texttt{center\_unembed=False}, \texttt{fold\_ln=False}.
Head ablation zeroes the head's $z$-vector at \emph{all} token
positions. MMLU uses the \texttt{cais/mmlu} ``all'' test split, a fixed
2{,}000-question subset (seed~0), 0-shot lm-eval format (subject
header, lettered choices, ``Answer:'' suffix), scored by a single
forward pass comparing the logits of \texttt{~A}--\texttt{~D} at the
answer position~\cite{hendrycks2021mmlu}; WikiText-2 uses the
\texttt{wikitext-2-raw-v1} test split, 100 non-overlapping windows of
1024 tokens taken sequentially from the joined test text, perplexity
from mean NLL over predicted tokens~\cite{merity2016wikitext}. The parity check between
TransformerLens and HuggingFace logits was conducted in float32
(mean KL $1.3\!\times\!10^{-5}$); all experiments run in bfloat16. KL
quantifies distribution-level divergence between the two framework
runs, not the
error of our summary metrics directly, so we additionally compared
logit differences, patching effects, and head rankings between the two
precisions on a fixed dev sample (Sect.~\ref{sec:results-localization}).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Behavioral landscape and localization}
\label{sec:results-localization}

On known facts, bare-prompt accuracy of \emodel{} is 100\%. Under
misleading context the competition is nearly balanced (47.3\%
following, 95\% CI 46.0--48.5, vs.\ 46.2\% truth) but strongly
template-dependent (24.2\%/98.4\%/19.2\%), matching reported
persuasion-strength variation~\cite{du2024context}; the neutral control
stays at 87.4\% truth with 1.2\% neutral-token copying
(Table~\ref{tab:behavior}). Template~1 (distractor adjacent to the
repeated question) drives following to 98.4\%, compatible with a
strong recency/copy contribution that this protocol does not isolate.
Reference benchmarks (lm-eval-harness): MMLU 49.4\%$\pm$0.4 (full
test set), TruthfulQA MC1/MC2 24.5\%/36.3\%~\cite{lin2022truthfulqa},
WikiText-2 PPL 65.3; the 2{,}000-question intervention subset yields
49.8\% (unified capability run); this is subset sampling, not protocol error.
\textbf{Precision robustness.} Repeating the LD measurements and
top-20 head-effect ranking in float32 vs.\ bfloat16 on the same 200
dev facts: mean $|\Delta\mathrm{LD}|$ is 0.07--0.08 logits vs.\ mean
$|\mathrm{LD}|$ 2.7--8.7 ($0.9$--$3\%$); head effects rank-correlate
at Spearman 0.99 (max per-head difference 0.008), and \spec{} ranks
first by mean restore effect among the compared heads on this
200-fact parity sample, under both precisions (the full-experiment
dev ranking in Sect.~\ref{sec:results-superposition} uses 150 facts
with per-head averaging over three suffix positions, hence its different leader,
L25.H4).

\subsection{Superposition and conflict specialists}
\label{sec:results-superposition}

Bidirectional patching (Fig.~\ref{fig:superposition}) shows every
influential head conveys the context more strongly than it restores the
memory ($B>A$; $S$ 0.21--0.62), consistent with head-level
superposition~\cite{li2025juice}. The neutral
control separates two populations, subject to a normalization caveat.
$N$ divides by the clean--neutral gap (Eq.\,(\ref{eq:N}); the
absolute value enters only the near-zero guard and magnitude reports),
which is far smaller than $D$ (median $|$gap$|$ 0.75 vs.\ 6.7 logits;
35\% of cases below 0.5; 5.3\% with $|$gap$|{<}10^{-6}$ set to
$N{=}0$), so $N/A$ is not a fraction of effect retained; in raw logit units, patching L25.H4's clean value
changes the neutral run by only 0.01 logits (0.50 in the misleading
run). Near-zero signed means are not mere cancellation: for the
\emph{median} case the patch leaves the output logits bit-identical in
bfloat16 (median $|\Delta\mathrm{LD}_N|{=}0$ for every top head,
95\% bootstrap CI $=[0,0]$; float32 medians are 0.002--0.007 logits,
so the exact zeros partly reflect rounding), with mean
$|\Delta\mathrm{LD}_N|$ of 0.03--0.08 logits driven by a minority of
cases. Two distinct thresholds are involved. \emph{Denominator}
thresholds (0.5/1/2 logits) change the $N$ estimate itself: cases
below them are zeroed, not dropped, leaving 291/186/72 of 450, under
which L25.H4's share stays largest ($N{=}0.029/0.019/0.006$ vs.\
$\le$0.016 for the specialists). \emph{Membership} criteria on
$|N|$ instead change which heads qualify: 4/6/9 heads pass
$|N|{<}0.005/0.01/0.02$, and \spec{} ($N{=}-0.006$) is selected
under the working criterion $|N|{<}0.01$ but would be excluded at
0.005. ``Specialist'' therefore means \emph{low neutral
effect under the tested control} (a point estimate;
Table~\ref{tab:neutral} gives CIs and the precision check), not
absence of other functions; its behavioral value is established by the

\subsection{Interventions and quantified trade-offs}
\label{sec:results-interventions}

Table~\ref{tab:dose} and Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff} report the held-out
interventions. Knocking out the dev-ranked top-$k$ heads reduces context-following
monotonically (19.3\%$\to$1.6\% at $k{=}20$), with overall accuracy
loss as $k$ grows (truth 74.4\% at none $\to$ 66.8\% at $k{=}1$
$\to$ 19.4\% at $k{=}20$, with a small non-monotonic bump at
$k{=}2$). The Stage-4 taxonomy predicts that removing a
conflict specialist suppresses the misleading pull at limited collateral
cost: knocking out the single
head \spec{} reduces hallucination to 12.6\% (two-sided exact McNemar
$b{=}81$, $c{=}1$, $p{=}3.4\times10^{-23}$) while \emph{increasing} accuracy to 79.7\% (+5.3 points;
the gain is itself significant: $b{=}81$, $c{=}18$, $p{=}1.0\times10^{-10}$)
and leaving neutral accuracy at 92.0\% (baseline 93.5\%; the 1.5-point
decrease is small but significant, $b{=}5$, $c{=}23$,
$p{=}9.1\times10^{-4}$). Fact-clustered 95\% CIs for \spec{}: follow
10.7--14.5\%, truth 77.5--82.0\%, neutral 90.4--93.5\%. By contrast,
ablating the five highest-norm heads \emph{increases} hallucination to
33.0\% ($b{=}9$, $c{=}173$): these are early/mid-layer high-$|z|$
heads (L2--L16), not the late-layer heads of Table~\ref{tab:heads}, and
their removal tips the final balance toward the context. The random \emph{five}-head ablation (random-5) is erratic across its
five seeds (follow 12.6--22.2\%, truth 44.8--78.8\%, inconsistent
sign and magnitude). For \spec{}, the paired
fact-clustered 95\% CI of the difference is $[+5.3, +8.1]$ points of
following removed and $[+3.7, +6.9]$ points of truth gained;
per-relation, following falls in 11 of 15 held-out relations
(median 4.2 points, range 0--16.7); broad but not relation-uniform;
all CIs are conditional on this fixed relation split.

\textbf{Why does almost any late-layer knockout help?} The dataset is
filtered to facts the model answers with 100\% bare-prompt accuracy,
so parametric memory is intact by construction. Perturbing any
attention head in the late layers (where our localization places the
context-conveyance pathway) weakens the in-context signal relative to
that intact prior, tilting the competition back toward the memorized
answer; consistent with the uniform behavioral improvement across
all ten random heads and with the contrast to topnorm-5, whose
early/mid-layer heads (L2--L16) damage the recall side instead and
\emph{increase} following to 33.0\%.

Capability costs are dissociated (Table~\ref{tab:cap}): MMLU retention
remains 0.99--1.01 for the patching-guided configurations evaluated
there (the random-1 summary row is excluded), and a paired
per-question comparison for \spec{} detects no change on this subset
(paired difference $-0.40$ points, 95\% CI $[-1.10, +0.30]$; $b{=}22$
gained, $c{=}30$ lost; exact $p{=}0.33$); no detected change, not
demonstrated equivalence; whereas perplexity rises 5--20\%. Among the patching-guided configurations, the single-head specialist
is the cheapest: a 35\% relative hallucination reduction, with an
accuracy \emph{gain}, at MMLU retention 0.992 and a 5\% perplexity
cost.

\textbf{Correct-context control.} A correct-context suite (T0
asserting the \emph{true} target, same held-out facts) tests damage
under context that is actually right. Baseline is near ceiling (98.2\%
truth); \spec{} leaves 96.1\% (small but significant, $b{=}0$,
$c{=}25$, $p{=}6\times10^{-8}$; far below its 5.3-point misleading-context
gain), whereas spec3 (81.6\%) and top-5 (60.2\%) destroy it. Because
this context concerns already-known facts, the result bounds damage
under consistent context rather than measuring context \emph{use} per
se; deployment beyond this counterfactual regime (e.g., general RAG
reliability) is out of scope.

\textbf{Template robustness.} Re-evaluating the none, \spec{}, and top-5
conditions on the two remaining held-out templates tests whether the
specialist's effect depends on how the context asserts the distractor. On T2 (user-hint
template) \spec{} again reduces following (16.6\%$\to$11.1\%) while
raising accuracy by 3.3 points, replicating T0. On T1 (the extreme adjacent-distractor template whose baseline
behavior is nearly pure context-following at 98.4\%, consistent with,
though not isolated by, a recency-copying route), \spec{} reduces
following from
98.4\% to 89.4\% (9.0 percentage points; 9\% relative) and raises
accuracy from 0.8\% (held-out baseline; the all-facts value in
Table~\ref{tab:behavior} is 1.1\%) to 8.7\%, while top-5 reduces
following from 98.4\%

additionally recruiting a copy pathway that a single specialist head
does not fully control. The intervention thus generalizes across
persuasion formats; residual following under T1 is consistent with a
copying contribution that the single-head approach does not remove.

\subsection{Cross-model replication}
\label{sec:results-cross}

Replicating the pipeline on Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen2.5-1.5B (600 known
facts each, split 144/456 and 184/416 dev/held-out by relation; coarse
and fine sweeps use the dev split only, capped at 300/150 facts)
confirms the core structural
and interventional findings, though the specialist taxonomy (Stage 4)
was run only for \emodel{} (Table~\ref{tab:cross}). First, late-layer
concentration holds in all three architectures: the most influential
coarse layers are 20--25 of 26 (\emodel{}), 13--15 of 16 (Llama), and
22--27 of 28 (Qwen), and the normalized-depth profiles rank-correlate at
0.81 (\emodel{} vs.\ each other model) and 1.00 (Llama vs.\ Qwen).
Second, baseline following varies across models (6.6\% and 14.9\%,
both below \emodel{}'s 19.3\%; the splits are relation-disjoint but not
item-identical; each model's filter retains different facts (456/416
vs.\ 1{,}193 held-out); so differences mix persuadability with data
composition).
Third, and most importantly, the single-head knockout from each model's
own dev ranking reduces distractor-following by 30\% (Llama:
6.6\%$\to$4.6\%, 95\% CI 2.9--6.6, exact McNemar $b{=}9$, $c{=}0$,
$p{=}0.004$) and 50\%
(Qwen: 14.9\%$\to$7.5\%, CI 5.0--10.1, $b{=}31$, $c{=}0$,
$p{=}9\times10^{-10}$) with small
accuracy changes ($-1.3$ and $+0.8$ points) and MMLU retention
1.01/1.00; the single-head effect replicates across architectures;
whether dev-ranked selection outperforms random single heads (as it
does not, on average, in \emodel{}) is not established for the
replication models, where no random baseline was run. Per-head restoration effects are smaller in the
1--1.5B models (0.04--0.06 vs.\ 0.08); this is consistent with a more
distributed conflict circuit at smaller scale, though the data do not
separate that reading from generally weaker per-head effects.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Mechanism.} Under our patching protocol, restoration
concentrates in late-layer attention outputs at the answer position,
and the influential heads convey the misleading context at least as
much as they carry memory ($B>A$
throughout). ``Restoration'' by patching therefore partly works by
displacing a head's context conveyance rather than injecting memory; the
neutral-control analysis makes this distinction measurable at the
level of patching effects between conditions; it does not by itself
identify the underlying pathway.

\textbf{Attention-sink specialists.} The most surprising finding is the
identity of the strongest conflict specialist: \spec{}, whose
answer-position attention is dominated by the beginning-of-sequence
token ($68\pm9\%$; the other specialists show only 22--35\%). In the
streaming-efficiency literature such sinks are treated as stabilizing
but semantically inert no-ops~\cite{xiao2024sinks}. Our results show
that a sink head can be causally significant here: \spec{} conveys the
strongest context push ($B{=}0.143$), and its removal improves accuracy
at MMLU retention 0.992 (PPL $\times$1.05). But
$N\!\approx\!0$ under one neutral-patching control only shows that this
intervention had minimal effect in the tested condition, not that the
head has no other function. The
mechanism by which a BOS-attending head influences the conflict remains
an open question. The BOS value vector itself cannot encode the
misleading context (which follows it in the causal mask); plausible
routes are a query-position output bias fed by the BOS value (the
write happens where the head queries, not at BOS), attention
redistribution, or downstream effects; and although
Figure~\ref{fig:attention}(b) aggregates 32 prompts, mechanism-level
claims need larger samples. What the results do establish is that
sink-focused heads should not be excluded from behavioral circuit
searches.

\textbf{Design implication.} The dissociation between MMLU (no
detected change on the tested subset) and perplexity (up 5--20\%)
suggests these heads support contextual integration that matters for
language-modeling loss more than for benchmark accuracy; and the
norm-selection failure warns that cheap importance proxies can worsen
the targeted behavior.

\textbf{Limitations and future work.} (i) English-only, greedy
completion-style evaluation; (ii) hallucination is operationalized as
counterfactual override, not open-ended factuality; (iii) head selection
is causal but the circuit description is coarse; path-level analysis is
the natural next step; (iv) \emodel{} is not the newest generation
(chosen for tooling maturity and literature comparability); newer
families and instruction-tuned variants are future work; (v) capability
is proxied by MMLU/PPL subsets; (vi) the Stage-4 specialist taxonomy ran
only for \emodel{}, and entity-popularity effects remain open.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We mapped and exploited the CM-conflict circuit of small language
models. Late-layer localization and single-head intervention
effectiveness replicate across all three architectures (following
falls 30--50\% under each model's dev-ranked head); the specialist
characterization itself is established only in \emodel{}, where
L22.H4, the sole head (of the four checked in both precisions)
passing the neutral criterion, can be knocked out individually, reducing
distractor-following by 35\% on the standard template (9\% on the
extreme adjacent-distractor template),
first-token accuracy rises for \spec{} (+5.3 points; the
replication models' top heads: $-1.3$ to $+0.8$) at MMLU retention
near 1.00, while norm-based selection backfires. Most random single heads
match or beat the specialist behaviorally at similar cost; a minority
degrade capability severely. The contribution is the causal
characterization and measured trade-offs, not a selection procedure
proven superior to random ablation. All selection was
performed on dev relations with results reported on held-out
relations (CIs are conditional on this fixed split),
and the core findings (late-layer concentration, single-head
intervention effectiveness) replicated across three architectures.
Split IDs, head lists, per-item records, analysis scripts, and
environment details accompany this submission as supplementary
material; the full experimental codebase will be released publicly
upon publication.
